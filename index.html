Your boss said “add UTMs”. Here’s what that means.

A UTM link is your normal link with a few labels added at the end. When someone clicks it, Google Analytics reads the labels and knows exactly where that visitor came from.

Example
example.com/sale?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=spring-sale
  • Source: who sent the visitor. Facebook, a newsletter, a poster.
  • Medium: what kind of traffic. A paid ad, a free post, an email.
  • Campaign: what you’re promoting. Spring sale, new course launch.

You don’t need to remember any rules. Answer 3 simple questions and the wizard builds a correct link for you. It takes about a minute.

Why can’t Analytics tell this by itself?

Sometimes it can, but often it can’t. Apps like Instagram, WhatsApp or email programs often hide where a click came from, so Google Analytics files those visitors under “Direct”, which means “no idea”.

UTM labels travel inside the link itself, so the information never gets lost.

Why not just write “facebook” everywhere?

Source says who sent the visitor. Medium says how. Google Analytics combines the two to decide which “channel” the visit belongs to, like Paid Social, Organic Social or Email.

Analytics matches the words exactly, so cpc and paid can land in different places. That’s why the wizard picks the words for you.

Campaign

What are you promoting?

Write it however you like. The wizard turns it into a clean, report-friendly name.

Tips for good campaign names
  • Use the same name on every channel, so Facebook, email and posters all add up into one campaign in your reports.
  • Add a date when the campaign repeats, for example black-friday-2026.
  • Don’t worry about capitals, spaces or Georgian letters. The wizard converts them to lowercase Latin letters with hyphens.
