Why can’t Analytics tell this by itself?

Sometimes it can, but often it can’t. Apps like Instagram, WhatsApp or email programs often hide where a click came from, so analytics tools file those visitors under “Direct”, which means “no idea”.

UTM labels travel inside the link itself, so the information never gets lost. That’s why they matter most for emails, QR codes and messaging apps like WhatsApp, where analytics usually sees nothing at all.

Something else: write your own source and medium
Why not just write “facebook” everywhere?

Source says who sent the visitor. Medium says how. Google Analytics combines the two to decide which “channel” the visit belongs to, like Paid Social, Organic Social or Email.

Analytics matches the words exactly, so cpc and paid can land in different places. That’s why the wizard picks the words for you.

Campaign

What are you promoting?

Write it however you like. The wizard turns it into a clean, report-friendly name.

Tips for good campaign names
  • Use the same name on every channel, so Facebook, email and posters all add up into one campaign in your reports.
  • Add a date when the campaign repeats, for example black-friday-2026.
  • Don’t worry about capitals, spaces or Georgian letters. The wizard converts them to lowercase Latin letters with hyphens.

What does the Doctor check?
  • Parameter names: typos like utm_campain and capitals like UTM_Source.
  • Values: capitals, spaces and symbols that split one campaign into several rows in reports.
  • Missing parts: source, medium and campaign must all be there.
  • Channel: whether Google Analytics will recognise the medium, and whether a social link is really a paid ad.

When there’s only one correct answer, the Doctor fixes it for you. When it depends on what you meant, it asks.
